Message

Message from the Director

Achieving carbon neutrality by 2050 is a major mission entrusted to science and technology. Yet the future we should be aiming for is not simply a society that reduces energy consumption and CO₂ emissions. What is required is a sustainable society that simultaneously enhances people's comfort, health, safety, and quality of life.

For more than fifty years, our research group has worked to understand different energy conversion systems—electrical, mechanical, thermal, and more—within a unified analytical framework based on circuit theory. Beyond theory and basic research, we have also developed real devices and systems, translating our findings into technologies that serve society.

Building on the system analysis and thermal engineering expertise we have cultivated, we are now taking on the challenge of establishing a new academic field, "Human Space Design Engineering," that connects architecture, mechanical engineering, information and control, energy, and human science. Using heat as a common language, we treat the spaces in which people live, work, learn, and move as a single system, and we are creating new design principles that achieve both comfort and health alongside energy conservation and decarbonization.

It is you—the students of today—who will lead society in 2050. We want to work together with those of you who wish to cross the boundaries of existing disciplines, take on challenges that have no established answers yet, and design the human spaces and society of the future with your own hands, to build new scholarship and technology together.

Kiyoshi Saito, Waseda University

Message from the Director (Full Text)

Achieving net-zero greenhouse gas emissions by 2050 is a major goal shared across the world. At the same time, decarbonization alone cannot bring about the bright future society we are aiming for. We must continue to develop industry and society sustainably while improving the richness, comfort, health, and safety of people's daily lives.

How, then, should science and technology confront such a difficult challenge?

Science and technology to date have advanced to a high degree within individual specialized fields—mechanical engineering, architecture, electrical engineering, information science, control engineering, human science, and others. However, the challenge of reconciling a decarbonized society with a life of genuine value for human beings cannot be solved within the conventional, vertically divided academic system alone. We need a new academic framework that connects the knowledge and technologies of different specialized fields and treats spaces and society as a single system.

For more than fifty years, our research group has shown, ahead of the rest of the world, that even different energy conversion systems can be analyzed and designed in a unified way by expressing them through circuit theory as a common analytical structure. Building on this theoretical foundation, we have advanced our research from basic studies all the way to the development, control, and demonstration of thermal energy devices and systems—turning theory into real-world technology.

What we aim to achieve next is to build a new academic field, "Human Space Design Engineering," on the foundation of the energy system analysis and thermal engineering expertise we have cultivated.

We call the spaces in which people live, work, learn, and move "human spaces." Human spaces encompass every place where people are active—homes, offices, commercial facilities, factories, mobility, and even the interior of clothing or outer space. Within these spaces, architecture, mechanical engineering, information and control, energy, and human beings interact with one another, generating value such as comfort, health, productivity, and safety.

One of the key physical phenomena at the center of it all is "heat."

Heat is a major factor in energy consumption and CO₂ emissions, while also being deeply connected to human comfort and health, product quality, production activities, and food safety. We must therefore stop thinking of heat merely as a loss to be reduced, and instead consider where it should be generated, how it should be transported, stored, used, and controlled, in order to create spaces of genuine value for people.

In Human Space Design Engineering, we use thermodynamics as a common language to connect architecture, mechanical engineering, information and control, energy, and human science. By making use of experiments, mathematical models, simulation, digital twins, AI, and optimal control, we aim to clarify the principles by which human spaces are formed, while building new design methods that achieve both comfort and health alongside energy conservation and decarbonization.

Our research is not limited to individual devices or buildings. We treat the entire chain as one system—from the supply side of society's energy, including renewable energy, distributed energy resources, hydrogen, and electricity and heat supply networks, to the demand side, including housing, workplaces, production sites, agriculture and food distribution, and mobility. Furthermore, our research spans everything from elucidating fundamental phenomena to developing devices and systems, demonstration, social implementation, standardization, and policy proposals.

What we aim for is not simply to learn existing disciplines, but to connect different fields and create scholarship and technology that has never existed before. This requires the ability to think beyond the boundaries of one's own specialty, the courage to take on challenges with no established answers, and a spirit of collaboration with one's peers.

Less than a quarter of a century remains until 2050. By then, it will be you who stand at the center of society. Whether we can achieve a decarbonized society and create a bright future in which people can live healthy, comfortable lives depends on you, who will carry science and technology forward from here.

Let us work together—starting from heat—to connect people, space, energy, and society, and to build a new field of scholarship that designs the human spaces of the future.

Kiyoshi Saito, Waseda University

Mission

Designing new human spaces that achieve both comfortable living and a decarbonized society

This is the ultimate mission of Saito Laboratory. The era of pursuing the performance of individual components alone—an air conditioner here, an insulation material there—is over. What the future demands is the integration of five elements—architecture, energy, mechanics, information and control, and human beings—to optimize the space itself as a single, unified system.

Founding Philosophy

The Founding of "Human Space Design Engineering"

We have boldly expanded the core knowledge of mechanical engineering to establish a new field: "Human Space Design Engineering." Using thermodynamics as a common language, we fundamentally redesign human space—composed of five elements—from a mechanical-engineering perspective built around analogy and related methods. Our goal is to develop innovative technologies that enhance comfort and health while realizing a decarbonized society.

Vision

Aiming to Become the MIT Media Lab

Just like the Media Lab, our Center aims to spark innovation that transcends conventional disciplinary boundaries. We aspire to become a hub of innovation that overturns the concept of spatial design, spreading from Japan to the world. In an international research environment that draws members from overseas, we welcome those of you who wish to join colleagues from around the world in tackling research on a truly large scale.
